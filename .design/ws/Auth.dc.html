job/auth.py · ingress_headers()

Two authentications, one request

The subtlest part of this socket. Two different things authenticate on the same handshake, and there is only one Authorization header to go round.

CHECK 1 The Databricks Apps proxy
Authorization: Bearer <Databricks OAuth token>
Authenticates who is calling — a service principal. It stands in front of the app and lets nothing through without one. That principal needs CAN_USE on the app.
CHECK 2 The app's own ingress check
X-DBX-App-Token: <DBX_APP_TOKEN>
Authenticates the job process, with the shared secret the app handed this run as a job parameter at trigger time. Not a Databricks identity — which is precisely why it cannot ride on Authorization.
Why the secret moved off Authorization
Put the shared secret there and the proxy rejects the handshake before the app ever runs — the run goes unobserved with nothing in the app's log to say so. The app still reads authorization as a fallback so the local dev stack, which has no proxy in front of it, keeps working unchanged.
THE FAILURE DOES NOT SAY WHAT IT MEANS
ws session ended (https://…/oidc/oauth2/v2.0/authorize?…
isn't a valid URI: scheme isn't ws or wss)
Not a bad URL. An unauthenticated handshake gets a 302 to the OAuth login page, the client follows it, and the error is about where it landed — a proxy asking a machine to log in through a browser. _diagnosis() in channels.py exists to translate exactly this one.
Where the OAuth half comes from
Four sources, first hit wins. Cached with a 300s refresh skew, because the HTTP-push channel sends on every flush and a token exchange per flush would be both slow and rude.
1 DBX_APP_OAUTH_TOKEN an escape hatch
2 client credentials the same /oidc/v1/token exchange the app does
3 DATABRICKS_TOKEN a personal access token
4 dbutils the identity the task already runs as — no secret anywhere
RIGHT CALL
None of it is fatal. A job that cannot authenticate logs why and runs without a live channel, exactly as it does when the app is simply down — the durable path never depended on the app being reachable.
